# Competition Pitch and Demo

## Structure (3 minutes, 7 slides max)

| # | Slide | Content | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hook | One user, one sentence, one number | 15s |
| 2 | Problem | Who hurts, how often, what it costs | 25s |
| 3 | Insight | Why existing solutions fail (one line each) | 20s |
| 4 | Demo | Live or video: the core loop, narrated | 75s |
| 5 | How it works | One architecture diagram, 5 boxes max | 20s |
| 6 | Proof | Metric, traction, or benchmark — real numbers | 15s |
| 7 | Ask / next | What you need, what happens next | 10s |

Slides support the talk; they are not the talk. No slide with more than 20 words.

## Demo script

Write it as a timeline with exact clicks:

```text
0:00 "Meet Sara, a clinic receptionist. Every day she loses 40 minutes..."
0:10 [click New Appointment] "She types the patient's name..."
0:25 [show conflict warning] "The system catches the double booking..."
0:40 [confirm] "Booked, with a WhatsApp reminder queued."
0:50 [switch to dashboard] "Here's the reduction: 40 minutes to 8."
```

Rules: seeded data, no typing live if avoidable, no dead ends, no "let me just...", and a recorded backup video at 1080p.

## Narrative rules

- One protagonist, one problem, one outcome. Not a feature tour.
- Numbers beat adjectives: "8 minutes instead of 40", not "much faster".
- Say the differentiator out loud, early and late.
- Name the hard technical problem you solved — judges reward evidence of depth.
- End on the ask, not on "thank you".

## Judging alignment

Before building slides, list the rubric criteria and assign each to a slide or demo moment. If a criterion has no moment, add one; if a slide serves no criterion, cut it.

## Q&A preparation (rehearse all of these)

1. How is this different from <big incumbent>?
2. How do you make money / who pays?
3. What happens at 100× the users?
4. What was the hardest technical problem?
5. What did you not build, and why?
6. What is your biggest risk?
7. Why now / why you?
8. How did you validate the need?
9. What is the data/privacy model?
10. What would you do with funding/time?

Answer format: direct answer (10s) → one supporting fact → stop. Never bluff; "we haven't tested that, here's how we would" scores better than invention.

## Delivery

- Rehearse twice with a timer; cut 20% of words after the first run.
- One presenter owns the demo machine; close notifications, zoom the browser, hide bookmarks.
- Have the app open and warm before you start; never build live.
- Watch the clock; if late, skip the architecture slide, never the demo.

## Anti-patterns

Reading slides, feature lists, unreadable diagrams, buzzwords ("AI-powered blockchain synergy"), demoing an empty state, and answering a question you do not understand instead of asking for clarification.
