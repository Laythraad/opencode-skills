# Product Strategy and OKRs

## Strategy before plans

A strategy is a **choice**: where to play, how to win, what not to do. If it does not exclude something, it is a wish list.

Template (one page):
1. **Situation** — the few facts that matter (market, users, capability, constraints).
2. **Where to play** — segment, geography, use case.
3. **How to win** — the durable advantage (distribution, data, cost, brand, switching cost).
4. **Capabilities needed** — what must be true internally.
5. **Bets** — 3–5 initiatives with expected outcome and kill criteria.
6. **Not doing** — explicit exclusions.

## OKRs that work

- **Objective**: qualitative, directional, memorable. 3 max per team.
- **Key results**: outcomes, not outputs. "Reduce p95 checkout to <800ms", not "ship caching layer".
- **Baseline → target → date**, with an owner per KR.
- Target ~70% attainment as success; 100% means the targets were too soft.
- Separate **committed** (must hit) from **aspirational** (stretch).

| Bad KR | Why | Better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch new dashboard | Output | 40% of active accounts use dashboard weekly by Q3 |
| Improve NPS | Vague, lagging | NPS from 31 → 40 among accounts >90 days |
| Increase revenue | No lever | Expansion revenue +15% via seat upsell in mid-market |

## Roadmapping under uncertainty

- **Now / Next / Later** beats a 12-month Gantt when uncertainty is high.
- Commit dates only for the near term and only for things with a design done.
- Every roadmap item: problem, expected outcome, confidence (high/med/low), size, dependencies.
- Reserve 20–30% capacity for maintenance, incidents, and debt — or it consumes 50% unplanned.

## Prioritization at portfolio level

Score with **value × confidence ÷ effort**, then override deliberately for strategic bets and dependencies. Keep a visible "why not now" list so rejected ideas do not resurface every quarter.

## Alignment mechanics

- One shared doc per quarter: strategy, OKRs, roadmap, owners.
- Weekly: metric review + blockers. Monthly: bet health (on track / at risk / kill).
- Kill criteria defined at kickoff: "if activation <20% after 8 weeks, we stop."
- Communicate decisions with the *reason*, not just the outcome.

## Anti-patterns

OKRs copied from the roadmap, 15 priorities, strategy that names no customer, quarterly planning that ignores capacity, and never killing anything.
