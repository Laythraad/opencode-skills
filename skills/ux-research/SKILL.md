# UX Research

## Choose the method by the question

| Question | Method | Sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What problem do users actually have? | Generative interviews | 5–8 per segment |
| Can they complete the task? | Moderated usability test | 5 per round, 3 rounds |
| Which of A/B is better? | A/B test or unmoderated test | Statistical power / 20+ for qual |
| How common is a behavior? | Survey / analytics | Power calculation |
| Where do they get stuck? | Session replay + funnel analysis | All traffic |
| How does the workflow work today? | Contextual inquiry / diary study | 5–10 |

Rule of thumb: qualitative finds *why* and *what*, quantitative finds *how much*. Never use 5 interviews to claim a percentage.

## Interview craft

- Ask about **past behavior**, not future intent: "tell me about the last time you..." beats "would you use...".
- Avoid leading and hypotheticals ("wouldn't it be easier if...").
- Follow the emotion: "you said it was frustrating — what exactly happened?"
- Silence is a tool; let them fill it.
- Never pitch the idea mid-interview; it contaminates the rest.
- Record (with consent) and take verbatim quotes, not summaries.

## Usability test script (25 min)

1. Warm-up (3 min) — context, no right answers, think aloud.
2. Tasks (15 min) — realistic, no UI vocabulary: "You want to send money to your sister. Show me how you'd do it."
3. Measure: completion, time on task, errors, and the **first point of hesitation**.
4. Debrief (5 min) — expectations vs reality, what they'd change.
5. Never help during the task; note where you wanted to.

## Synthesis

1. Tag observations (not opinions) from notes/replays.
2. Cluster into themes; a theme needs ≥3 independent instances.
3. Separate **observation → interpretation → implication**; keep them in distinct columns.
4. Produce: top problems ranked by frequency × severity, evidence quotes, and one recommended change per problem.
5. State confidence and what would change your mind.

## Artifacts that survive contact with teams

- **JTBD statement** and a job map, not a fictional persona with a stock photo.
- **Journey map** with emotion curve + pain points tied to evidence.
- **Usability report**: task, success rate, top 5 issues, severity, clip timestamps.
- **Research repository** entry: question, method, sample, findings, decisions it influenced.

## Ethics and logistics

Informed consent, right to stop, no dark patterns in recruiting, anonymize recordings, and compensate participants. Store PII separately from findings.

## Anti-patterns

Research as theater after the decision, asking "do you like it?", testing with colleagues, sample of one segment applied to all, and reports with no prioritized action.
